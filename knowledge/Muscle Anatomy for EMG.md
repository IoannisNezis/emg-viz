# Muscle Anatomy for EMG

Surface EMG placement requires knowing the target muscle's anatomy. These are the most commonly measured muscles.

## Upper Limb

| Muscle | Location | Action | Notes |
|--------|----------|--------|-------|
| **Biceps brachii** | Anterior upper arm | Elbow flexion, forearm supination | Most common teaching muscle for EMG |
| **Triceps brachii** | Posterior upper arm | Elbow extension | Often measured as antagonist to biceps |
| **Flexor carpi radialis** | Medial forearm | Wrist flexion | Used in grip/grasp studies |
| **Extensor digitorum** | Lateral forearm | Finger extension | Used in hand gesture recognition |
| **Deltoid** (anterior/middle/posterior) | Shoulder cap | Shoulder flexion/abduction/extension | Three distinct heads, three electrode sites |
| **Trapezius** (upper) | Upper back/neck | Shoulder elevation, head rotation | Common in ergonomics (computer use) |
| **First dorsal interosseous** | Hand, between thumb and index | Index finger abduction | Small but accessible, used in fine motor studies |

## Lower Limb

| Muscle | Location | Action | Notes |
|--------|----------|--------|-------|
| **Tibialis anterior** | Anterior shin | Ankle dorsiflexion | Easy to measure, strong signal |
| **Gastrocnemius** (medial/lateral) | Posterior calf | Ankle plantarflexion | Two heads, often both measured |
| **Vastus lateralis** | Lateral thigh | Knee extension | Part of quadriceps, easy placement |
| **Rectus femoris** | Anterior thigh | Knee extension, hip flexion | Crosses two joints |
| **Biceps femoris** | Posterior thigh | Knee flexion | Part of hamstrings |
| **Gluteus maximus** | Buttock | Hip extension | Large muscle, strong signal |

## Trunk

| Muscle | Location | Action | Notes |
|--------|----------|--------|-------|
| **Erector spinae** | Parallel to spine | Trunk extension | Often measured in back pain studies |
| **Rectus abdominis** | Anterior abdomen | Trunk flexion | ECG crosstalk is common here |
| **External oblique** | Lateral abdomen | Trunk rotation | |

## Electrode Placement Principles

Per SENIAM guidelines:
- Place electrodes on the **muscle belly** (midpoint between tendon and muscle origin)
- Align electrodes **parallel to muscle fiber direction**
- **Inter-electrode distance**: 20 mm (center-to-center) for bipolar configuration
- Avoid placing over the **motor point** (innervation zone) — signals are less stable there
- Avoid the **tendon** region — lower amplitude, more crosstalk
- **Reference electrode** (ground) on a bony prominence (e.g., elbow, wrist, ankle)
